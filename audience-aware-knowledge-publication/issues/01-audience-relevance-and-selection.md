# Define audience relevance and source selection

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Labels: wayfinder:grilling

## Question

What exactly makes an authorized Artifact relevant enough for an Audience's Knowledge Projection?

## Comments

### Resolution

The Audience Access Policy determines the complete captured input set. An authorized Artifact cannot be silently excluded as “irrelevant”; the Audience brief guides grouping, synthesis, placement, granularity, and retrieval usefulness. Every captured Artifact must be a direct provenance dependency of at least one Knowledge Variant, but it need not receive its own Variant or standalone section.

Authorization and coverage remain Artifact-level. A partially visible Memory is valid: denied Artifacts behave as absent, and the curation model receives only eligible source material for the target Audience. Exact duplicates may be represented by one Variant with all relevant provenance; near duplicates may be grouped by the model without discarding source identities.

The retained evidence is the captured policy/brief version, source IDs and content hashes, generated Variant source sets, and redacted audit metadata. Prompts, raw provider responses, denied source identifiers, and source text are not retained as audit evidence.

Decide how the Audience curation brief is interpreted, whether relevance is assessed per Artifact, Memory, or candidate topic, and how a Memory with both eligible and denied Artifacts is represented. Cover ordinary source updates, multiple related Memories, irrelevance, duplicate or near-duplicate material, and the boundary between deterministic authorization and model-assisted editorial selection. The result must define what source material can be shown to a curation model and what evidence is retained for the selection decision.
